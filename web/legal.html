Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 2026 · Applies to dropX on Android, iOS, Windows, macOS and Linux.

dropX sends files directly between your own devices over a local network. It has no servers, no accounts and no analytics. We (the developer) never receive your files or any information about how you use the app.

At a glance

  • Works only over local area networks (LAN / Wi-Fi) and peer-to-peer device hotspots.
  • No servers, cloud storage or intermediate relay nodes — files go straight from one device to the other.
  • Every transfer is end-to-end encrypted between the two devices, with a 6-digit verification code shown on both screens.
  • No tracking pixels, no advertising identifiers, no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting.
  • No account or sign-up. Nothing is sold, rented or shared with advertisers or data brokers.
  • Support emails you choose to send are used only to reply to you, and are never added to any profiling database.

1. How dropX works

dropX operates exclusively over local area networks (for example, your home Wi-Fi) and over peer-to-peer hotspots created by one of your devices. Devices find each other on that network automatically, then connect to each other directly to transfer files.

dropX does not operate any servers, and no transfer is routed through the internet, a cloud service or a relay node controlled by us or anyone else. An internet connection is not required for dropX to work.

2. Information we do not collect

The developer does not collect, receive or store any personal data through the app. In particular, dropX does not:

  • contain tracking pixels, analytics or telemetry software development kits (SDKs);
  • access or use advertising identifiers (such as Google Advertising ID or Apple IDFA), or show advertisements;
  • send crash reports, usage statistics or diagnostics to us automatically;
  • upload, scan or inspect the contents of the files you transfer;
  • determine, record or share your location;
  • require an account, email address or phone number.

3. Information stored only on your device

To work, dropX keeps a small amount of information locally on your device. It never leaves your device except as described in section 4.

  • Display name — the name you choose (or your device's default name) so other devices can recognise you.
  • Transfer history — for each transfer: the other device's display name, file names, total size, date and time, whether it succeeded, and where received files were saved. You can delete individual entries or the whole history at any time in the app.
  • Settings — such as your language and your chosen save location on desktop.
  • Received files — saved to the location you choose (or, on iOS, your Photos library for photos and videos, when you allow it).

Uninstalling dropX removes its stored data from your device (files you received and saved elsewhere remain where you saved them).

4. Information shared with nearby devices

While dropX is open, it makes your device discoverable to other dropX devices on the same local network. To do this it announces your display name, your device type and operating-system version (for example, Android 14 or Windows) and the dropX version. This announcement is not encrypted, because other devices need to read it to find you; anyone on that same network running compatible software may be able to see it. Choose a display name you are comfortable with others on that network seeing.

When you send files, the device you choose to send to receives the files themselves and their names and sizes. Nothing is shared until you select files and a receiving device, and a transfer only takes place between the two devices involved. Everything exchanged during a transfer is end-to-end encrypted (see section 8).

dropX asks only for the permissions it needs to find nearby devices and move files. You can deny or revoke any of them in your device's settings; some features may then stop working.

6. Diagnostic logs you choose to share

To help troubleshoot failed transfers, dropX keeps a short technical log of the current session in memory only; it is not saved to disk and is discarded when the app closes. Nothing is sent automatically.

If you choose “Share Log”, dropX first shows you the exact text to review. Before it is displayed, IP addresses, device host names and your computer's account name are replaced with placeholders (such as IP_1, HOSTNAME_1 and USER). File names and dropX display names may still appear; the log never contains the contents of any file. It leaves your device only if you choose Download or Send yourself, to whoever you choose.

If you choose to send it to the developer, dropX opens a draft email to support@dropx.dev in your own email app, with the log attached and a short summary of the problem filled in (such as the error shown, the app version and your device's operating system). You can read and edit the draft, and nothing is sent unless you send it. Section 7 explains how support emails are handled.

7. Support emails

If you contact us by email, we receive your email address and whatever you include in your message (for example, a description of the problem, screenshots or a log you chose to attach). This information is used strictly to read and reply to your technical support request. It is not added to any profiling or marketing database, not used for advertising, and not sold or shared with third parties. We keep support correspondence only as long as needed to resolve your request, and you can ask us to delete it at any time.

8. Security

Because transfers never pass through our servers or the internet, your files are only ever on the two devices involved and on the local network between them.

End-to-end encryption. For every connection, both devices generate brand-new encryption keys. These keys are never stored and never leave either device. Every file, file name and transfer message is then encrypted and authenticated, so other devices on the same network, including the router itself, cannot read or alter what you send. In addition, dropX verifies that every received file arrived intact.

Verification code. Before a transfer starts, the sending and receiving devices both show the same 6-digit code. If the codes match, the encrypted connection is directly between your two devices. If they do not match, reject the transfer.

Encryption protects what is sent, not who sends it. Only accept transfers you expect, from devices you recognise. Devices running a dropX version released before encrypted transfers were introduced cannot connect to current versions, so update dropX on all your devices.

9. Children's privacy

dropX does not collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13 (or the equivalent minimum age in your country).

10. Your choices and rights

Because the developer holds no personal data from the app, there is nothing for us to access, correct, export or delete on our side. Everything dropX stores is on your own device and under your control: you can clear transfer history in the app, change your display name, revoke permissions in system settings, or uninstall the app. For support emails, you can ask us to delete your correspondence using the contact details below. These choices apply regardless of where you live, including under the GDPR, UK GDPR, CCPA/CPRA and India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act.

11. Third parties

dropX does not include third-party advertising, analytics or tracking services. It is built with the open-source libraries listed in Open Source Attributions, which run entirely on your device. If you download dropX from an app store, the store's own privacy policy applies to that store.

12. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version ships with a new version of the app and is posted on this page, each with a new “Last updated” date. The same policy is always available in the app under Settings → Legal & Privacy, with no internet connection needed.
